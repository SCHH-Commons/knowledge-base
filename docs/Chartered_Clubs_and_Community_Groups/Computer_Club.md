# Computer Club

The mission of the Computer Club is to provide our members the opportunity to enhance their knowledge of the latest computer technology our Club has to offer. Our fully equipped state-of-the-art facility, along with the many classes, lectures, special programs, meetings, equipment and so much more, fulfill this mission. Staying abreast of technological developments through “Learning & Sharing” are continuing goals of the Computer Club!

## Membership

### Who can join the Computer Club?

The Computer Club is open to all Sun City residents. 
For questions about joining or your membership, email schhccmembership@gmail.com 

### Do I need to own a computer?

Although owning a computer will be helpful to practice your newly learned skills in the comfort and convenience of your own home, it is not necessary. Computers are available for your use in the Open Room at the Computer Club facilities.

### Membership provides opportunities for:

- Socializing with peers who share your interest in computers and related technolog y Learning basic computer skills without fear or expanding on what you have already learned
- Sharing your skills and knowledge with your peers
- Learning more about technology and how it can bring you closer to family and friends

## A Brief History of Sun City’s Computer Club

Sun City Hilton Head opened its gates in June 1995, and the first 100 residents moved into their new homes that August. Shortly thereafter, in 1996, the Computer Club was created. The Computer Club is one of the largest clubs within Sun City and over the nearly three decades of its existence it seems apropos to look at the history of the Computer Club, the Presidents, and the facilities that compromise its current existence. Many people that started the club have come and gone and much of the history that is described is based on word of mouth and hearsay, but the intent is to describe as best we can, the major elements of the Sun City Hilton Head Computer Club history.

### Our Facilities

The club was formed in September 1996. The club initially met in the left half of the building that is currently the Sewing Club. Eight PC computers were purchased by Del Webb to start the club’s equipment; all equipment purchased after that was paid with funds from membership fees. The club started with 29 members (May 1, 1996), but within a few months the membership had grown to 300+ members. As Sun City has grown so has the membership of the Computer Club to greater than 3,000. The club moved to its current facility in August 2008. Classes, help sessions, and general computer use occur in our two-room facility, in the Yemassee Craft Center, located in Town Center. One room is used for classes, lectures and help sessions while the other room provides members the full use of Apple, PC and other state-of-the-art computer equipment, printers, and scanners; these devices allow our members evaluate the latest alternatives to computing prior to purchasing a home device. They also allow members to use the equipment if they don’t have thei r own equipment. Equipment for copying VHS tapes to DVDs and digitizing 8 mm andv super 8 movie film as well as small accessories like a USB floppy drive, DVD copier and a hard disk docking station are available.

### Committees and SIGs

Once the Computer Club became established, as it matured a series of Committees were put in place; many of these committees are still in place while others have disappeared due to the advancement of technology and current relevance:

- Education 
- Monitors
- Membership 
- Newsletter (no longer in existence)
- Product Review (no longer in existence) 
- Programs
- Technical (no longer in existence) 
- Web Page
- Room Administrators

In 2000, the membership fees were $25 per person; in 2022, the membership fees were lowered to $20 per person and $35 per couple. In 2024, the fees are $10 per person and $5 for a second person per household. Total income in 1997, the first full year of operation, was $2,764. In 1998, the income was $12,784 and in 2002 it was $19,286; in 2024, the income is >$30,000. These funds are used to upgrade, replace, and maintain the computers and support equipment that are used daily. In addition to Committees, there were Special Interest Groups (SIGs) established to meet the needs of the club. • Apple SIG shares information on the latest Apple trends and provides education and assistance regarding Apple computers, iPhones, iPads and Apple Watches – i.e. all things made or for Apple products. • Microsoft SIG follows the leading-edge Microsoft developments and provides help and education with these products. • Genealogy SIG members hone their research skills and learn about the many resources available in their quest for discovering their family’s history. • Sky Watchers SIG focuses on understanding our universe through space science and astronomy. • The Computer Club Hour SIG meets twice monthly (except for the summer months) with speakers presenting timely, cross platform topics. Sessions are designed to introduce new tools, inspire curiosity, and encourage further exploration in computing related topics. SIGs come and go depending on the technology innovations and maturity and of course the willingness of volunteers to establish and maintain the SIG. Volunteers and Our Presidents

Our volunteers are the lifeblood of the club who willingly share their expertise on all levels and enjoy sharing their computer skills with other members:

- Instructors provide up-to-date information in small classes pertaining to the new digital world in which we live.
- Class assistants work alongside instructors during classes to assist students with maximizing their learning while gaining hands-on experience.
- Monitors open and maintain the security of the computer room and provide answers to many questions members and guests have about the club and technology.
- Helpers provide one-on-one assistance to members who are experiencing challenges with using and maintaining their portable Apple, Windows and Android devices.

Facilities Committee members’ responsibilities include maintaining the equipment, purchasing new equipment and updating software. The Computer Club operates under a set of Bylaws, originally drafted in 1996, and evolved over the years. Currently, there are 4 Officers (President, Vice President, Secretary, and Treasurer), 2 Board Members at Large, and a Past President that vote on issues that affect the future sustainability of the club. The Computer Club annually recognizes a member who has contributed to the club's success. Starting in 2007, the Rasmussen Award, named after an early member of the Club, Robert Rasmussen, who contributed greatly to the Club's formation and growth, was created. Bob Rasmussen brought to Sun City a wealth of skills. Although he contributed to the community in general, it was at the Computer Club where he shared both his technical and organizational talents. Although he has left the area, the legacy of his help and service remains and a deserving member receives the honor of receiving the Rasmussen Award. Additionally, over the years, individual volunteers were recognized each month as Volunteers of the Month.

### Summary

From the small beginnings in 1996, a year after Sun City Hilton Head opened, the Computer Club has grown from 29 members to over 3,000 members and from 8 computers to >25 computers and other devices. The growth has been spurned by the increase in population in Sun City, but also by the dedication and commitment of our volunteers.
