# SCHH Landscape Glossary & Explanations

## Landscape Services

### Service

This is referred to a scheduled visit to perform any or all maintenance services.

### Mowing

- Mowing can be referred to mowing of entire lawn.
- Selective mowing means that certain areas may be mowed and others may not.  
Circumstances to ‘not mow’ can include: areas that are too wet, recently installed sodded, or areas requested not to be mowed.

### Hard Edging

- This is performed with every other mowing service. Areas will include: sidewalks, driveways, and patios.
- A stick edger with a blade is used for this tasks.

### Soft Edging

- This is performed along landscape bed edges (mulch or pine needles) to keep a clean cut delineation between turf and bed. It also helps control encroaching turf from invading the bed.
- This is performed with a line trimmer or ‘weedeater’. Plastic line is used.

### Bed Weeding

- This is performed in ‘developer installed’ beds only. Meaning, these are the original beds in front of your home that were installed at the time of construction.
- Performed either by hand removal or by chemical control.

### Crack Weed Removal

- This is referred to weeds that are growing within the expansion joints of concrete; sidewalks and driveways.
- The removal and control process is performed by use of a line trimmer and by chemical use.

### Blowing

- Blowing is performed after every mowing service and on occasion when mowing doesn’t occur. Areas include: sidewalks, porches, patios, and driveways.
- A backpack blower is utilized for this task.

## Landscape Issues**

### Turf Scalping

- Defined as mowing down to bare dirt.
- This can have several culprits, for example:
     - Uneven blades on mower
     - Uneven topography in lawn
     - Straddling drainage easements
     - Mowing around utilities where boxes/ lids are above grade

- Solutions
     - Allow the turf to repair on its own. Typical timeline for repair: 2-3 weeks.
     - If necessary, re-sod the damaged areas.

### Turf Ruts

- This is where the mower wheels sink into saturated soil leaving a depression in the lawn.

- Solution
     - Fill in with soil and re-sod

### Turf Tracking

- This is very typical and normal especially when there are periods of rain and mowing operations occur.
- The ‘tracking’ is caused by dirt being picked up by the mower wheel and left on top of the grass.
- Does not cause any long term damage.
- Solution
     - Wash off tracks and allow the area to dry.

### Turf Insects: Mole Crickets, Army Worms, etc.

- This is an ecological issue that is not guaranteed each year.
- These can be treated with a ‘contact’ insecticide which will kill the insects instantly.
- ‘Systemic’ insecticides can be used to help provide residual protection against any potential insect issue.

### Turf Fungus (Brown Patch –most common)

- Humidity, Heat, and Rain are contributing factors to fungus forming.
- Centipede turf is the most susceptible turf to have fungus during the growing season.
- Solutions
     - Apply monthly preventative fungicide treatments to help safeguard your turf.
     - Summit will apply a 1x application per year, if needed, to all turf areas affected by fungus But does not include spot treatments or individual service. Summit will apply by treating all lawns.
     - Apply fungicides immediately upon detection. If you treat immediately, this will help stop the fungus from spreading to more areas in your lawn.
