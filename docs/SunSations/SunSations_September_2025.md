# SAFETY REMINDERS FROM THE CHIEF OF SECURITY
*by Joe Schetting, Securitas Chief of Security*

It’s hard to believe that summer is winding down already. Soon the weather will be cooling down and more residents will be out and about on the roadways and trails throughout Sun City Hilton Head. I want to remind residents of some important safety issues and provide tips to help keep everyone safe as we swing into autumn.  

As the community continues to grow, so does our volume of traffic. Because of this, it is important for residents to remember how critical it is to adhere to the rules of the road.  

I have spoken about this previously, but Sun City is a unique community as far as transportation goes. Golf carts and bicycles are used regularly for everyday transportation, along with vehicles on the roadways. This brings uncommon driving challenges when traveling throughout the community. Because golf carts and bicycles go significantly slower than vehicles, it’s even more important to adhere to the posted speed limits and stop at Stop signs. Remember to give yourself extra time to get where you need to be, rather than trying to pass a golf cart or bicycle in a dangerous area.  

September is Stop Sign Awareness month in Sun City, and our patrols will be paying close attention to intersections to ensure vehicles come to a complete stop. The small amount of time it takes to come to a stop is well worth it for the safety of everyone on our roads.  

Patrols have been seeing electric bikes using trails and sidewalks more frequently. This is a friendly reminder that electric bikes are not permitted on trails, sidewalks or golf course paths throughout the community.  

The new golf cart and pedestrian bridge crossing over Argent Boulevard opened recently. It is important to remember to walk on the left side, facing oncoming traffic on the bridge. Signage and some other improvements will be made soon to increase safety awareness on the bridge.  

Residents also need to be aware of safety issues beyond the roadways. Because scams continue to be reported within Sun City, in May, the Association hosted the Beaufort County Sheriff’s Office for a scam presentation. I strongly encourage anyone that did not attend the presentation to take the time to view the presentation slides. To find it online:  

- Log into **www.SunCityHiltonHead.org**  
- Scroll to the Community Library tile.  
- Click Documents.  
- Expand the Other Presentations folder.  
- Click on *Scams-What You Need to Know May 2025*.  

Scams continue to evolve and become more elaborate; anyone can become a victim. Be vigilant and if something does not seem right, do not be afraid to contact law enforcement or Security for guidance.  

It is also important to remember that historically, September and October are typically our busiest months of hurricane season. During Sun City’s annual Severe Weather Symposium in June, our public safety partners from Beaufort and Jasper counties presented great information that residents should be aware of when there are impending threats of a hurricane. To watch the video of the Hurricane and Severe Weather Symposium:  

- Log into **www.SunCityHiltonHead.org**  
- Scroll to the Hurricane Awareness tile and click Learn More.  
- Scroll to Updates and click Learn More.  
- Scroll to the bottom of the page and find the link to the Hurricane and Severe Weather Symposium under the Hurricane Preparedness Presentations section.  

In addition, be sure to explore the Hurricane Awareness page at **www.SunCityHiltonHead.org** for even more resources. The Hurricane Preparedness Guide includes a wealth of information on how best to prepare for an impending hurricane and keep your family safe before, during and after a storm.

# Stay Safe and Informed with Everbridge Nixle  
*by the Sun City Hilton Head Community Association*  

Everbridge Nixle is a free message alert system used by Beaufort County and Jasper County officials to notify the public about severe weather events, road closures, traffic obstructions and other public safety incidents. Additionally, both counties use Everbridge Nixle to communicate with the public when a hurricane threatens the area.  

Sun City Hilton Head residents are strongly encouraged to sign up for Everbridge Nixle alerts to ensure you receive the most reliable and up-to-date information directly from the source, which is especially important during hurricane season and a hurricane evacuation.  

Residents can sign up to receive text message alerts, email alerts and/or mobile app alerts regardless of your phone and internet carriers. Read on for instructions to opt-in.  

---

## 📲 Register for Text Message Alerts  
The easiest way to opt-in to receive Everbridge Nixle alerts is to text your zip code to **888777**.  

1. Open your messages.  
2. Start a new message.  
3. Type the number **888777** in the “To” line. Then in the message box, type your zip code to begin receiving local alerts.  
4. Once you type your zip code into the message box, you will see an arrow appear. Press the arrow to send the message.  
5. After you send the message, you will get a text confirming that you are enrolled. You will now receive text alerts from local law enforcement officials.  

---

## 📧 Register for Email and Text Alerts  
1. Visit **[https://local.nixle.com/register](https://local.nixle.com/register)**  
2. Fill out the online form to supply your email address and phone number.  
3. Click **I Accept. Sign me up!**  

---

# 📱 Download the Everbridge App  
The free Everbridge app, available for iPhone and Android users, allows users to look up alerts and safety information and customize the information by what matters most to you.  

To prevent duplicate information, users will receive mobile app notifications instead of texts for low-priority events.  

To download the app for new users:  
1. Open your phone’s app store.  
2. Search for “Everbridge.”  
3. Select the app with the blue icon to download and install it on your device.  
4. Once downloaded, click allow to receive notifications and alerts.  
5. From the app, input your zip code in the search bar at the top to see all available organizations that are relevant to you.  

### Subscribe for information from:  
- Beaufort County Sheriff’s Office  
- City of Hardeeville  
- Jasper County Emergency Services  
- Jasper County Sheriff’s Office  
- SC Department of Public Safety  
- Town of Bluffton  

---

## 🔄 For existing Everbridge Nixle subscribers:  
From the app, existing users can link existing text message subscriptions from Everbridge Nixle to your profile in the app.  

1. After downloading the app, click **Start Here** where it asks if you are already receiving text alerts.  
2. Enter your phone number and the verification code it sends to you.

# ART IN BLOOM 2025  

*by Lisa Armbruster, contributing writer

Now in its fourth year, Art in Bloom promises to delight once more with beautiful and inspiring pairings of paintings and fresh floral arrangements. Don’t miss this unique event on **Saturday, September 6 from 10 a.m.–4 p.m.** and **Sunday, September 7 from 10 a.m.–2 p.m.** at the Yemassee Crafts Center, Art Studio. Art in Bloom is a collaboration between the All About Art and Avant Gardeners clubs. The process begins with the artists who submit their works of art for the show. The floral designers of Avant Gardeners are then teamed up with the artists to provide a floral interpretation of the art.  

The artwork is presented alongside the floral designs at the exhibit. The floral arrangements may be a re-creation or an interpretation with texture and color capturing the essence of the art.  

Barbara Radvillas, president of Avant Gardeners, said, *“It is amazing to see the talent showcased by Avant Gardeners and All About Art club members during this unique show. Last year, as a first-time floral designer, I enjoyed interpreting my assigned artwork. The best part was understanding the artist’s viewpoint before tackling the floral arrangement.”*  

As you enter the Art Studio, your senses will be awakened by a bouquet of lovely floral scents. Touring the display, you will see a variety of art and floral styles, themes, colors and textures reminding everyone once again of the amazing talent within the community.  

All About Art club president Patti Rozonokiewicz invites all Sun City residents to visit the show. *“Don’t miss the best-smelling Art show ever! I hope everyone checks it out,”* she said.

# FIND YOUR PLACE IN THE Ballroom Dance Club  

*by Dennis McCafferty, Ballroom Dance Club member and contributing writer  
Photos courtesy of the Ballroom Dance Club*  

---

So, you’re thinking about getting into ballroom dancing? Whether you’re a fresh-faced newbie or a seasoned pro with a dusty pair of dance shoes, the Ballroom Dance Club has the perfect place for you to kick up your heels.  

Take Doug and Lyn M. for instance. Lyn always dreamed of dancing, while Doug was just trying to be a good sport and keep Lyn happy. He agreed to learn how to dance if Lyn agreed to learn to play golf. So, a few years ago they waltzed (literally) into the Ballroom Dance Club ready to give it a shot.  

They started with Friday night dances, and after only a few $3 professional group lessons (that’s less than a fancy coffee!), they discovered a secret treasure trove: free coaching lessons on Saturdays. They also found out there are free Monday beginner classes. It was like the universe was telling them to dance their way to happiness, a lot of new friends, and a lot of new friends.  

After a few months, with determination (and maybe a little bit of desperation to avoid stepping on each other’s feet), they managed to nail the basics of waltz, cha-cha, and East Coast Swing. But Lyn wasn’t done yet—she dragged Doug to the free Tuesday Latin classes and considered taking the free Wednesday Carolina Shag classes. Three years later, they’re dancing like they were born with rhythm, making friends, and secretly competing with each other to pull off the best cha-cha move.  

**Moral of the story?** If you put in time, you’ll see progress. Dancing is no different than pickleball or golf—the more you practice, the more fun you’ll have. And let’s face it, nothing says “living your best life” like being able to dance to random tunes in your living room.  

Now, let’s talk about Bob and Connie Grande. These two had already been dancing for years, but they hit a snag when they moved to Sun City. Between unpacking boxes and exploring the Lowcountry, they forgot about dancing. Cue the rusty ballroom shoes and the desperate longing for the good ol’ days. But like a shining ball from the disco ball, they found the Ballroom Dance Club! With weekly dances and plenty of opportunities to refresh their skills, they were ready to shake off the dust.  

Bob and Connie are now back to the groove with Tuesday Latin classes, Wednesday Carolina Shag lessons, and, because they were feeling fancy, even gave the Sunday Country Dancing and two-step a whirl. After all, why not add a little boot-scootin’ boogie to the mix?  

Bob and Connie are back in their dancing prime, meeting new friends, and living their best lives as proud members of the Sun City dance squad. They’ll tell you, “The best part about dancing is getting back into it—and maybe learning a new step or two along the way.”  

So, whether you’re a seasoned twirler or someone who’s only recently learned that “Cha-Cha” isn’t just a type of candy, there’s a place for you to dance your heart out. Just remember, if you dance hard enough, you might even forget you’re working out.

# STEP INTO THE FUN  
## Square Dancing is for Everyone  

*by Sam Rowan, Sun City Squares member and contributing writer  
Photos by Sally Hamilton and Donna Campagnola*  

---

Did you ever want to have fun on the dance floor, but discovered you have two left feet and no rhythm? Then square dancing with Sun City Squares might be for you.  

In square dancing, it doesn’t matter what foot you start with, and the caller guides you through the moves so that dancers don’t even have to keep the beat.  

Each time the music begins (the club covers a wide variety of music styles and genres), every dancer becomes part of a “team” of eight people (a square) who smile, laugh and work together to successfully make it back “home” (your starting position—not your mailing address) with your partner beside you.  

Sun City Squares president Ken Kurski said, *“Square dancing is a lot of fun. I have met some of my best friends at square dancing. It engages you mentally and physically.”*  

Club secretary Julie Donelson added, *“What makes a club such as Sun City Squares so successful is the support of our board members, class coordinators, members and our fantastic caller. Come join us, get off that couch, and meet some amazing people and get some exercise!”*  

---

In April 2024, Sam Rowan, the club caller, welcomed 102 new members into the Sun City Squares, which is still the largest square dance club in South Carolina. With more than 40 years of calling, Sam makes dancing fun while also being patient as both new and seasoned dancers alike.  

One new dancer, Trudy Higgins said, *“Square dancing is fun and a great activity. But most of all, the club members are so helpful and encouraging to a beginning dancer. I’m happy I made the step to join in the fun.”*  

Cheryl Ward and her friend recently completed the Mainstream Square Dance class that they started last October. *“Each Thursday night has been an evening of socialization, exercise and laughter, as well as excellent exercise for our brains,”* they said. *“It is good to take the simple act of walking—no fancy steps, just walking to a whole new place. This miraculously happens with the help and guidance of our very skilled caller and his collection of fun music. I am so glad to have learned the basics and to have the Sun City club weekly activities for practice, learning and many more smiles.”*  

Another new dancer, Larry Gottlieb, was reluctant to start square dancing at first. But his wife, Mindy, convinced him to give it a try as a couple’s activity, along with some close friends by their side. *“What I found is a welcoming community, friendship and a challenge,”* he said.  

Larry thinks of square dancing in two parts—walking and knowing where to walk. *“Walking is easy,”* he said. *“Knowing where to walk is not as easy. However, it is a great way to challenge myself as a way to keep my brain active and sharp. I had no idea about how welcoming and friendly the square dance community is. Every week we all laugh at ourselves along with everyone else as we learn called after call. I look forward to learning more and continuing in fun square dancing.”*  

Mindy Gottlieb added, *“Square dancing is something we do together; it is our date night. No phones, TV distractions. We walk, dance and laugh. Sam is a pro when it and such a great teacher. We learn and laugh for two hours each week. And there is always candy! I love how welcoming everyone is.”*  

In addition to the regular Tuesday night fun, the club holds special theme events like St. Patrick’s Day, Halloween, several patriotic evenings, Western night, Jimmy Buffet and Cinco de Mayo and more. But the usual dance attire is casual and comfortable. There is also a group that regularly pays it forward by dancing for assisted living facilities in the area. There are even two special interest groups (SIGs) including the Oregon Sticks and the Irish Folk Dancing SIG.

# One Ring, Two Love Stories  
*by Joe Turino, SunSations team*  

---

The ring is more than metal and stone. It’s a circle—unbroken and endless—symbolizing love that lasts, promises that endure and the quiet ways lives connect. Though worn on the hand, its true meaning is held in the heart.  

And for one Sun City Hilton Head couple, an engagement ring has come to be not just their own love story, but the enduring power of love itself.  

---

When Charlie and Florika Richardson got engaged in 2013, the diamond ring Charlie offered her carried a story that began years earlier. It had once belonged to a woman named Sandy, someone Florika never met. At first, the story gave her pause. But what started with uncertainty soon became something more: a symbol of love continued.  

Sandy had been engaged to a man named Tim. On the morning of September 11, 2001, she was working inside the Pentagon when American Airlines Flight 77 struck the building. In the days that followed, 125 people from the Pentagon along with all 64 people on the plane were mourned. Sandy was among them.  

After many months, amid the wreckage and grief, Sandy’s engagement ring was recovered and returned to Tim. Years later, in early 2013, facing financial hardship, Tim made the difficult decision to sell it.  

That’s when Charlie heard the story from a mutual friend who knew Tim. Deeply moved, Charlie wanted to help. Purchasing the ring was his way of offering support during a difficult chapter. He was dating Florika at the time and thinking quietly about their future together, but the decision to buy the ring wasn’t about that—not yet.  

---

When the time came to propose, Charlie shared the ring’s story with honesty and care. He told Florika everything and made it clear that if she felt unsure, he would find another ring, no questions asked.  

Florika took time to reflect and the story stayed with her. When she shared it with a trusted neighbor, she received quiet encouragement: *“Maybe it’s God’s will that the ring continues to live.”* Those words opened her heart. She accepted the ring and said, *“yes.”*  

Charlie and Florika were married in 2015. This year, they celebrated their 10th wedding anniversary. Today, she wears her ring not only as a sign of their love, but as a symbol of renewal, resilience, and of love that carries forward in unexpected and beautiful ways.  

And in a twist of fate, just one year after selling the ring, Tim won $1 million in the Virginia State Lottery. Some may call it coincidence. Others see something more—a quiet reminder that life, like love, often finds its own way forward.  

---

The ring that began one love story now lives at the center of another circle once forged in promise, now renewed in devotion. It rests on Florika’s hand but lives in something deeper: the belief that love, like the ring itself, has no beginning and no end. Passed from one heart to another, it carries more than memory. It carries love that lasts.  

# Sun City and the Arnold Palmer Cup  

*by Rick Burke, SunSations team  
Photos by Sun City Volunteers*  

---

Residents of Sun City Hilton Head seem to have a few guiding principles: One is to enjoy their hobbies to the fullest, and to volunteer their time, expertise and talent to those who ask for help. Both of those were evident in early June when avid Sun City golfers volunteered their time and experience to help the Arnold Palmer Cup run smoothly.  

Seventy golfing residents spent six days in the hot, humid weather manning different stations in an event that pitted American collegiate golfers against collegiate golfers from around the globe. The event was held June 2–7 at the Congaree Golf Club in Ridgeland. The highly regarded course has Lowcountry features of sand dunes and swamps, and is a challenge to any golfer.  

The purpose of the event, sponsored by the Arnold Palmer Foundation, is to promote youth character and further the growth of amateur and collegiate golf.  

> “I was overwhelmed by the quality of golfers,” said Sun City resident volunteer Steve Courcy. “I was impressed by their level of play and how mature they presented themselves. They were an impressive group of kids.”  

---

## Tournament Format  
The tournament mirrored the format of the Ryder Cup by utilizing match play. In fact, past participants have gone on to play in the Ryder Cup.  

Each team, the U.S. and the International, consisted of 12 men and 12 women. The tournament was comprised of three stages.  

- **Stage 1: Mixed Four Ball**  
  A match of 12 groups of four players each. Each member of the team plays their own ball. The lowest player score counts for the hole, with the winning team scoring a point.  

- **Stage 2: Mixed Foursomes**  
  Two golfers compete by alternating shots until the hole is halved, with each team scoring one ball.  

- **Stage 3: Singles**  
  One player from each team plays against another, with the higher total number of wins taking the match.  

---

## Sun City Volunteers Step Up  
Congaree didn’t have the staff to fully run a tournament. The Arnold Palmer Foundation would provide logistical support, but the tournament needed boots on the ground.  

Tom Craft, Congaree’s Director of Golf, approached Sun City Director of Golf Operations Charlie Kent for help. Charlie immediately sent out an email blast to the community’s roughly 4,000 golfers asking for volunteers. An experienced team of 70 volunteers was assembled (from the overwhelming nearly 200 golfers who offered to help). At least 60 of those 70 had previously worked at amateur or professional tournaments.  

Charlie Kent explained:  
> “People here in Sun City are very much in a helping mentality.”  

Volunteers were divided into five groups: Walking scorers, parking, shuttles, supply and cadets/vice scorers. Each group had a scorer who walked with the group and tallied the number of shots taken. A group of 12 volunteers worked in two shifts using golf carts to shuttle personnel around the course. They kept each hole supplied with water, snacks and protein drinks.  

Although no Sun City residents caddied for the event, they were responsible for looking after the needs of the 48 local high school students recruited to caddy. Most of the high schoolers had no previous experience with golf.  

---

## Lasting Impact  
The International team bested the U.S., but the ultimate winners might just be the Sun City volunteers. They were given tournament caps, shirts, lunch, snacks and a future round of golf at Congaree. The club will hold a volunteer appreciation day in September to honor the work of the volunteers by giving them a banquet and the opportunity to play the premier course.  

The best reward, however, might be the opportunity to serve others and make new friends.  

> “I got to meet and spend time with people I never knew,” said John Shea. “I got to see some of the best golf there is. The players were premier golfers.”

It first appeared Off-Broadway where it had a five-year run, was the third longest-running musical and the highest-grossing production in Off-Broadway history.

What grows into a way for the lonely Seymour to earn money and get close to his boss and coworker, Audrey, soon turns sinister. The plant, intent on conquering the human race, begins to eat humans! Seymour is forced to choose between staying true to himself or giving in to temptation.
