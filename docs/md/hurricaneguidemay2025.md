n October 2016, Sun City Hilton Head experienced its first hurricane in its 21-year history when the Lowcountry
was struck by Hurricane Matthew. Aside from some flooding, downed trees and extensive damage at the Nature
Trail, Sun City was mostly spared the devastation seen in most of Beaufort and Jasper counties. However, it was

#### I

a stark reminder of the serious threat hurricanes pose to this slice of paradise.


###### .........................................................................................3


A hurricane is a tropical cyclone that is accompanied by thunderstorms and a counterclockwise circulation of winds
that can reach 75 mph or higher. Hurricane intensity is measured in categories 1-5, with 5 being the strongest.

As much damage as Hurricane Matthew caused as a Category 2 hurricane, it could have been much more
devastating. Hurricanes can cause tornadoes, flooding and storm surges that can result in catastrophic property
damage, financial loss and injuries. Even a tropical storm can cause destruction and prompt an evacuation of the
area.

Weather experts are able to track the development and path of hurricanes and tropical storms, allowing us plenty of
time to prepare and leave. The Community Association has a plan to protect the assets of the community. We hope
residents will take the same precautions to protect themselves, their loved ones and their property. Do not wait until
a hurricane is on its way; planning ahead is the only way to ensure the safety of your family, pets and property.

Much of the information in this guide comes from the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), www.fema.gov
and www.ready.gov; the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Agency (NOAA), www.noaa.gov; The National Hurricane
Center (NHC), www.nhc.noaa.gov; local emergency management officials and the South Carolina Emergency
Management Division, www.scemd.org.

###### Evacuation: Leave Early

he South Carolina governor may issue an
evacuation order for certain counties based on
the projected track of the hurricane. If a storm

#### T

threatens the coastline, stay tuned to local news
outlets and government agencies to find out if an
evacuation order is issued for the area and for further
instructions.

One of the most important things to remember about
an evacuation is to leave early! Do not wait until right
before the storm hits as you could be stuck in traffic
for hours. Plan to leave during daytime hours and let
someone know where you are going. After you leave
the community, you may be required to follow the
South Carolina Highway Patrol’s evacuation traffic
pattern. Make sure you know how to get to your
evacuation location.

If the governor has not yet issued an evacuation order
but the storm is heading our way, consider leaving the
area to stay ahead of traffic.

**During an evacuation order, all inbound gates**
**into Sun City will remain open. If you leave the**
**property, you may be subject to follow evacuation**
**routes set by local authorities, which means you**
**may be prevented entry into Sun City.**


###### Emergency Shelters:
 A Last Resort

Public emergency shelters will open if an evacuation is
ordered. Shelters should be a last resort when con­
sidering an evacuation location. Shelters often do not
accept pets due to health regulations. If you plan to
use a shelter, bring the following items:

- Change of clothing (enough for several days)

- Linens (pillows, blankets, sheets and towels)

- Any necessary prescription medications

- Entertainment and/or comfort items

- Food and water

###### Get Out of Harm’s Way

There will be no Community Association or emergency
services available after an evacuation order is issued.
Residents are not advised to “ride out” a hurricane.
Hurricanes can also bring severe weather conditions
such as tornadoes and flooding that could lead to
dangerous situations and injuries. Security will not be
on the property, and it is possible that all the local
hospitals will be closed until authorities deem the area
safe for return. Depending on the storm’s impact, it
could be several days before hospitals are open and
emergency services are available again.

###### Other Safety Tips in the
 Aftermath

       - Be careful when walking around outdoors and
watch out for downed trees and power lines,
debris and displaced animals such as snakes and
alligators.

enter a building surrounded by floodwater.

###### Plans For Architectural Review Board Guidelines

In case of a major disaster, the Modifications Committee has adopted procedures to expedite project
review and approval.

 - If you are replacing or rebuilding part of your property that was present prior to being destroyed, you do
not need modifications approval to rebuild.

 - You do need approval for any new additions or changes.

Since Sun City Hilton Head may not be fully accessible in the aftermath of a hurricane or natural disaster,
Modifications Committee members will not be required to visit each site. Instead, members may review and
approve design plans electronically sent to them at off-property locations. As always, the Modifications
Committee and Community Association management intend to work closely and cooperatively with each
landowner to restore a safe and secure living environment.

###### Evacuation When Sun City Hilton Head is under an evacuation order, the only way to exit the community will be through the Lake Somerset Gate, North Gate, Riverbend Gate and Sun City West Gate.

 All inbound gates will remain open during and after an evacuation.
 After exiting Sun City, you may be subject to evacuation routes set by
 local authorities.

 Millennium Bridge will be closed during an evacuation to help with internal evacuation routes and for wind safety precautions.

 Sun City Lane will be closed at the intersection of Sun City Blvd and
 Del Webb Blvd.

 Residents living in neighborhoods south of Hwy. 278 Follow the traffic pattern shown in the map when in Sun City and exit out of the Lake Somerset Gate. You will turn left onto S.C. 170 and follow it north to S.C. 462/I-95.

 Residents living in neighborhoods north of Hwy. 278 Follow the traffic pattern shown in the map when in Sun City and exit out of the North Gate. You will turn right onto Hwy. 278 and follow it west to I-95.

 Residents living north of Hwy. 278 in Sun City West Exit out of the Sun City West Gate. You will turn left onto Argent Blvd. At the intersection of Argent Blvd and S.C. 170 you will turn left onto S.C. 170 and follow it north to S.C. 462/I-95.

 Riverbend residents Exit out of the Riverbend Gate. You will turn right onto S.C. 170 and follow it north to S.C. 462/I-95.
