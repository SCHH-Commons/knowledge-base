# Chartered Clubs and Registered Community Groups

Living in Sun City Hilton Head is all about embracing the lifestyle this community offers, which includes a lively, robust and exciting social scene constantly buzzing with activity. Sun City’s Chartered Clubs and Registered Community Groups provide residents with endless ways to meet friends, play sports, get creative, give back to the greater community and so much more.

With more than 100 options, there is sure to be a Chartered Club or Registered Community Group that caters to every interest or hobby. From casual card games to gardening, stamp collecting to line dancing, sports to philanthropic organizations, it can all be found within these pages.

Now in its fifth year, the Club Catalog has become a popular resource for newcomers and long-time residents when navigating the club and group options. While every issue of *SunSations* magazine contains a Chartered Club and Registered Community Group News Directory, the Club Catalog is meant to provide useful and more in-depth information for each club and group in one publication.

This edition includes the most up-to-date directory of Sun City’s clubs and groups, as well as officer contact information, instructions on how to join or renew membership, and organization descriptions. Viewing this publication online allows you to hover and click on the links to navigate directly to the club and group webpages for even more information.

Turn to the back of this issue to find additional resources and information pertaining to clubs and groups in Sun City, including how to receive the twice-monthly Clubs and Groups eNewsletter and how to establish a new club or group.

Be sure to download this issue to your computer or print it at home and keep it handy to reference year-round. We hope everyone finds something in these pages to help you fulfill your active adult lifestyle. As Sun City celebrates its 30th anniversary this year, there is sure to be even more fun ahead! For more information about clubs and groups, please contact Lifestyle Services at (843) 705-4027.

## Additional Resources and Information

### How to Sign Up for the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter

Keep track of Sun City's club and group community-wide events with the twice-monthly Clubs and Groups eNewsletter! Delivered to subscribers on the second and fourth Wednesday of the month, this eNewsletter includes an overview of the upcoming Chartered Club and Registered Community Group events that are open to all Sun City residents. Residents must be subscribed to eNews Weekly to receive the newsletter.

#### Signing up is easy!
1. Log into [www.SunCityHiltonHead.org](https://www.SunCityHiltonHead.org).
2. From Resident Central, scroll to the Account Management tile and click "Opt-In eNewsletters."
3. Fill out the form. To receive the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter, check that option at the bottom of the form.
4. Click "Subscribe."

##### Current eNews Subscribers
If you currently receive eNews Weekly but do not receive the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter, please see below for opt-in instructions:
- Scroll to the bottom of any eNews Weekly or The Week Ahead email and click the "Update Your Preferences" link.
- Click "Email Me a Link" on the next window.
- You will receive an email with a link to update your subscriptions. Click "Update Your Preferences" in the email.
- Select the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter. Then click "Update Profile."

### How to Submit Information for the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter

The Clubs and Groups eNewsletter is published on the second and fourth Wednesday of each month. The deadline for inclusion is noon on the Monday prior to publication. Email [communications@schhca.com](mailto:communications@schhca.com) with event details in the body of the email or as a Microsoft Word attachment.

#### Submission Guidelines
- Clubs and groups may promote four different events per year in the Clubs and Groups eNewsletter.
- Each of the four events may be promoted twice in the two issues of the eNewsletter prior to the event.
- Events must be open to all residents and held inside Sun City.
- Submissions should be no more than 100 words.
- Any Special Interest Group wanting to use one of the four events must obtain written approval from its parent club.
- Submissions cannot solicit members.

### Explore the Club and Group Webpages

Every Chartered Club and Registered Community Group has its own webpage at  
[www.SunCityHiltonHead.org](https://www.SunCityHiltonHead.org).

#### To find club and group webpages:
- Log into [www.SunCityHiltonHead.org](https://www.SunCityHiltonHead.org).

- Scroll down to find a club based on its category.

### Editing Your Club or Group’s Webpage

Club and group webpage administrators can update and edit club and group webpages on  
[www.SunCityHiltonHead.org](https://www.SunCityHiltonHead.org).

#### To find tutorials about editing webpages:
- Visit [www.SunCityHiltonHead.org](https://www.SunCityHiltonHead.org).
- Click the "FAQs" button at the top of the page.
- In the Table of Contents, look for the Tutorials section and click "Website Editing Tutorials for Clubs, Groups, Neighborhoods and BACs."

For questions about your club or group's webpage editing permissions, please call Lifestyle Services at (843) 705-4027.

### Establishing a New Chartered Club or Registered Community Group

Are you interested in starting a new Chartered Club or Registered Community Group? Be sure to contact the Lifestyle Services Department at (843) 705-4027 for all the information you need to get started. Staff will guide you through the steps of establishing your organization. Here is some helpful information:

#### Chartered Clubs
- Must maintain a roster of 100 or more active Sun City residents.
- Only Sun City residents can become members.
- Are allowed to require members to pay dues to generate revenue.
- May not set certain preconditions for membership or be tied to vocations, religion, politics, specific geological regions, etc.

#### Registered Community Groups
- May include non-resident members, but the total membership must be two-thirds, or 67 percent, Sun City residents, with at least 40 members total.
- May be formed based on social, service, political, geographical, vocational, or educational interests.
- May only have Sun City residents acting as board members.

#### Clubs or Groups in Formation
Clubs or groups that are not yet established and looking to secure membership may contact Lifestyle Services first. At the direction of Lifestyle Services, the organization may be listed as a club or group in formation in the Club or Group News section of *SunSations*. The word limit for groups in formation is 25 words, and they may be listed in the magazine for up to three consecutive months.

## A Brief History of Sun City’s Computer Club

Sun City Hilton Head opened its gates in June 1995, and the first 100 residents moved into their new homes that August. Shortly thereafter, in 1996, the Computer Club was created. The Computer Club is one of the largest clubs within Sun City and over the nearly three decades of its existence it seems apropos to look at the history of the Computer Club, the Presidents, and the facilities that compromise its current existence. Many people that started the club have come and gone and much of the history that is described is based on word of mouth and hearsay, but the intent is to describe as best we can, the major elements of the Sun City Hilton Head Computer Club history.

### Our Facilities

The club was formed in September 1996. The club initially met in the left half of the building that is currently the Sewing Club. Eight PC computers were purchased by Del Webb to start the club’s equipment; all equipment purchased after that was paid with funds from membership fees. The club started with 29 members (May 1, 1996), but within a few months the membership had grown to 300+ members. As Sun City has grown so has the membership of the Computer Club to greater than 3,000. The club moved to its current facility in August 2008. Classes, help sessions, and general computer use occur in our two-room facility, in the Yemassee Craft Center, located in Town Center. One room is used for classes, lectures and help sessions while the other room provides members the full use of Apple, PC and other state-of-the-art computer equipment, printers, and scanners; these devices allow our members evaluate the latest alternatives to computing prior to purchasing a home device. They also allow members to use the equipment if they don’t have thei r own equipment. Equipment for copying VHS tapes to DVDs and digitizing 8 mm andv super 8 movie film as well as small accessories like a USB floppy drive, DVD copier and a hard disk docking station are available.

### Committees and SIGs

Once the Computer Club became established, as it matured a series of Committees were put in place; many of these committees are still in place while others have disappeared due to the advancement of technology and current relevance:

- Education 
- Monitors
- Membership 
- Newsletter (no longer in existence)
- Product Review (no longer in existence) 
- Programs
- Technical (no longer in existence) 
- Web Page
- Room Administrators

In 2000, the membership fees were $25 per person; in 2022, the membership fees were lowered to $20 per person and $35 per couple. In 2024, the fees are $10 per person and $5 for a second person per household. Total income in 1997, the first full year of operation, was $2,764. In 1998, the income was $12,784 and in 2002 it was $19,286; in 2024, the income is >$30,000. These funds are used to upgrade, replace, and maintain the computers and support equipment that are used daily. In addition to Committees, there were Special Interest Groups (SIGs) established to meet the needs of the club. • Apple SIG shares information on the latest Apple trends and provides education and assistance regarding Apple computers, iPhones, iPads and Apple Watches – i.e. all things made or for Apple products. • Microsoft SIG follows the leading-edge Microsoft developments and provides help and education with these products. • Genealogy SIG members hone their research skills and learn about the many resources available in their quest for discovering their family’s history. • Sky Watchers SIG focuses on understanding our universe through space science and astronomy. • The Computer Club Hour SIG meets twice monthly (except for the summer months) with speakers presenting timely, cross platform topics. Sessions are designed to introduce new tools, inspire curiosity, and encourage further exploration in computing related topics. SIGs come and go depending on the technology innovations and maturity and of course the willingness of volunteers to establish and maintain the SIG. Volunteers and Our Presidents

Our volunteers are the lifeblood of the club who willingly share their expertise on all levels and enjoy sharing their computer skills with other members:

- Instructors provide up-to-date information in small classes pertaining to the new digital world in which we live.
- Class assistants work alongside instructors during classes to assist students with maximizing their learning while gaining hands-on experience.
- Monitors open and maintain the security of the computer room and provide answers to many questions members and guests have about the club and technology.
- Helpers provide one-on-one assistance to members who are experiencing challenges with using and maintaining their portable Apple, Windows and Android devices.

Facilities Committee members’ responsibilities include maintaining the equipment, purchasing new equipment and updating software. The Computer Club operates under a set of Bylaws, originally drafted in 1996, and evolved over the years. Currently, there are 4 Officers (President, Vice President, Secretary, and Treasurer), 2 Board Members at Large, and a Past President that vote on issues that affect the future sustainability of the club. The Computer Club annually recognizes a member who has contributed to the club's success. Starting in 2007, the Rasmussen Award, named after an early member of the Club, Robert Rasmussen, who contributed greatly to the Club's formation and growth, was created. Bob Rasmussen brought to Sun City a wealth of skills. Although he contributed to the community in general, it was at the Computer Club where he shared both his technical and organizational talents. Although he has left the area, the legacy of his help and service remains and a deserving member receives the honor of receiving the Rasmussen Award. Additionally, over the years, individual volunteers were recognized each month as Volunteers of the Month.

### Summary

From the small beginnings in 1996, a year after Sun City Hilton Head opened, the Computer Club has grown from 29 members to over 3,000 members and from 8 computers to >25 computers and other devices. The growth has been spurned by the increase in population in Sun City, but also by the dedication and commitment of our volunteers.

### Bid Whist Club

A Fun and Easy Card Game to Play. Bid Whist is a variant, played in the United States, of the classic card game, whist. As indicated by the name, bid whist adds a bidding element to the game that is not present in classic whist. It is generally accepted that the game of bridge came from the game of whist.

### Canasta Club

The Canasta Club meets Thursdays and Sundays from 12:30 to 4 PM in the Yemassee Craft Center Card Room across from Purrysburg Fitness Center. We welcome all residents who play any form of Canasta. If you have a home game, bring your team and join the fun. There are many variations of Canasta played by our members. We offer lessons to members for Super Samba on the 2nd Thursday of every month.

### Okatie Creek Duplicate Bridge Club

We welcome all bridge players interested in a friendly competitive bridge game. We are a sanctioned ACBL club awarding Master Points. We play on Mondays 1–4:15 p.m., Tuesdays 9 a.m.–12:15 p.m. (2 games per month are team games) and Saturdays 9 a.m.–12:15 p.m. Hands are pre-dealt. Results and analysis of hands played are posted on the website following games. If you need a partner, check out our website. Check the webpage listed above for details about lessons and special events.

### Pinochle Club

We play double deck for Move-Up and Non Move-Up games (Move-Up Games consist of changing partners and winners advance to the next table... Non Move-up consists of staying on one table and playing with your same group throughout the night). Move-up game begins at 6 p.m.
