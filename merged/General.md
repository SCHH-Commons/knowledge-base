# BOD Updates

### BOD Update: August 11 Working Session Recap


The Board of Directors held a working session on August 11. The following agenda items were discussed:  
  
**General Manager Position**  
AAM has posted the General Manager position in various locations, listing the extensive qualifications required to apply. AAM will gather the applications, do an initial screening and submit applicants to the Board for a pre-screen interview. The Board will then select at least three candidates for further consideration. In-person interviews will be scheduled for these applicants with three separate groups: the Board, select members of the Board Appointed Committees and a select group of staff members.  
  
**Golf**  
The Board members met with Director of Golf Operations Charlie Kent, Director of Golf Maintenance Rick Barnes and key leadership from Troon to discuss a number of issues, including progress on the Okatie Creek Golf Course renovations and the conditions of the Hidden Cypress Golf Course.

Charlie Kent published the following rule changes to golfers last week and they are shared here so that all residents are aware of the changes to take place Monday, September 1.  
  
“To support long-term course quality and enhance the overall golfing experience, the Board of Directors, in coordination with the Golf Advisory Committee, has approved changes to golf cart usage."   
  
**Effective Monday, September 1:**  
Hidden Cypress and Argent Lakes Golf Courses will transition to shared cart usage, limiting foursomes to two carts. Single golfers must pair up in one cart. On cart path only days, shared carts are required to maintain pace of play.  
  
**Effective Wednesday, October 1:**  
Okatie Creek Golf Course will adopt the same shared cart policy upon reopening.  
  
**Why the Two-Cart Policy Matters**  
We recognize that changes to long-standing practices can be challenging. However, this decision was made with careful consideration and a focus on long-term benefits for both the courses and the golfing community.

Key Benefits:   
• Improved Turf Health: Reducing cart traffic helps minimize wear and tear on fairways, roughs and bridges.  
• Enhanced Playing Conditions: Less soil compaction leads to healthier turf conditions and better overall course quality.  
• Faster Pace of Play: Shared carts by all groups help maintain a steady flow, reducing delays and backups.  
• Environmental Responsibility: Fewer carts mean lower fuel use and emissions, supporting sustainability goals.  
• Cost Efficiency: Reduced cart usage lowers maintenance costs and allows for reinvestment in course improvements.  
• Consistency Across Courses: A unified policy simplifies operations and ensures a consistent experience for all players.  

**Golf Advisory Committee Recommendations**  
Members of the Golf Advisory Committee (GAC) presented four recommendations to the Board for consideration. The Board will discuss the recommendations and take them under advisement. One recommendation was to support Charlie Kent’s policy change to permit the use of two carts instead of four which will go into effect Monday, September 1. The GAC also recommended that this change be evaluated in mid-2026 to discuss the effects it has had on course conditions. The GAC recommended that the Board consider moving the renovation of Hidden Cypress Golf Course forward from the current projected date of 2029. This will be taken under consideration during the budget process this year.  

**Bluffton Township Fire Station #39**  
The sign indicating that Fire Station #39 is progressing has been installed on Sun City Boulevard. It is not in front of the area where the station will be, but rather north of the gate to Okatie Farms East. This was done to allow land disturbance to progress without constantly moving the sign.  
  
**Change in Community Rules**  
The following change will be made to the Community Rules and filed with both counties as a part of the Community Association's Governing Documents. The changes go into effect as soon as a UWC is signed this week.   
  
Section 9.1. Skateboards, hoverboards and electric scooters are not permitted on any sidewalks leading to Association facilities and recreation areas, parking areas or trails throughout the community. Skateboards, hoverboards and electric scooters may not go upon any roadway designed for vehicular traffic posted more than 25 miles per hour except while crossing a street at an intersection. Skateboards, hoverboards and electric scooters used on any roadway shall always be ridden to the furthest right side of the roadway and shall not obstruct the flow of traffic at any time. Skateboards, hoverboards and electric scooters are not permitted on any roadway from sunset to 30 minutes past sunrise. It is recommended to wear retro-reflective clothing or gear as well as helmets when riding a skateboard, hoverboard or electric scooter. For purposes of this section, skateboards include both manual power and motorized. Electric scooters include both stand up and seated models.

**South Carolina Department of Natural Resources**  
Three representatives from the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources (SCDNR) spent time with the Board and management sharing information about alligators, how the state manages the population, recommendations for resident education, etc. Morgan Hart, Alligator Project Leader; Sam Chappel, Assistant Chief of Wildlife; and Will Dillman, Assistant Chief of Wildlife, presented information.  
  
There are over 100,000 alligators in lower South Carolina, about one million in Florida and over two million in Louisiana. Alligators considered to be aggressive are removed and euthanized. They reiterated that residents should never feed alligators (throw food in lagoons, etc.) or move birdfeeders far away from a lagoon (these attract rodents, small animals, etc. that then attract alligators). They recommended that residents visit the [state webpage on Alligators in South Carolina](https://dnr.sc.gov/wildlife/herps/alligator.html) for more information. SCDNR is releasing a new site called Gator Wise in two weeks. [Click here](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/hwi/vol17/iss3/12/) to read about new research in Florida about alligators.

### Association Update: Alligator Safety

Alligators are moving around the community this time of year, so please be aware of your surroundings. Please review the information below to brush up on alligator safety.  
  
Alligators are a part of the natural landscape within Sun City, and their presence in community lagoons signals a healthy ecosystem. Although an alligator in a lagoon or on the bank of a lagoon may not be aggressive, children and pets should not go near the water’s edge of lagoons.  
  
Please do not feed the alligators as it is illegal and dangerous. Alligators usually become aggressive after they are fed by humans. If an alligator sees humans as a food source, it has a reason to approach them and could become aggressive, meaning it will have to be removed from the community and destroyed.

Homes that have bird feeders close to ponds attract birds and other small wildlife including deer, raccoons and rabbits. Alligators might approach these yards to capture prey.  
  
If you’re spending time fishing, the best practice for baiting your hook is to face the water. Do not turn your back to a pond. Those who are golfing should be alert near lagoons. Be cautious when trying to scoop balls out of the water.  
  
If you think you have an aggressive alligator on your property, contact Common Area Maintenance at 705-6358 during business hours or Security at 705-4083 after hours. They will review the situation and determine the best course of action.  
  
Please note that alligators can only be removed by a qualified and licensed individual who must obtain a permit from the South Carolina Department of Natural Resources. To obtain the permit, the reason for the alligator removal must be specified. Alligators cannot simply be relocated because they will find their way back to the area from which they were removed.

Remember to stay alert and remember these best practices while enjoying the outdoors this spring. For even more information, [click here](https://vimeo.com/410628706?utm_source=Sun+City+Hilton+Head+eNews&utm_campaign=6cea31e57f-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_2025_01_20_07_34_COPY_01&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_-d93e869e0c-234469029) to watch a video on co-existing with alligators in the Lowcountry.

# Sun City West NR FAQ

Here are some "Frequently Asked Questions" that we have answered over time from our residents. All are worth reviewing. ENJOY!

## 1. What's the difference between Pulte -BOD - CAM? Please differentiate the role of each and the impact each has on what happens here in Sun City.

SCHHCA - stands for Sun City Hilton Head Community Association. The Association is comprised of EVERY homeowner in our community. We own everything that has been deeded over to us from the developer. That is, we own over $1 86 M of assets that are ours to enjoy, maintain and renovate.

BOD - Board of Directors is comprised of 3 Pulte members, 2 elected resident members, and 2 Pulte appointed "alternates". The Board is responsible for setting policy and assuring the governing documents are followed. Among other things, we supervise the management companies, oversee the financials and the budget, respond to residents, and plan for the future.

Board members have a "fiduciary responsibility" to the residents. The resident Board members have an obligation to take off our homeowner hats and put on a hat as an officer of the HOA. We are charged with using our best business judgment when making decisions which is the judgment that best applies to the facts we are dealing with and protects the community.

There are three components here:
➢ The Board has a duty of care which is to make informed decisions. We need to know and understand our state laws for HOAs, CC&Rs and other governing documents, and must act in a prudent and reasonable manner, using sound business judgment.
➢ The Board has a duty of loyalty which means we must act fairly, in good faith, and for the benefit of the Association as a whole. Decisions cannot be based on personal interest or gain, and any conflict of interest needs to be avoided.
➢ The Board has a duty to act which means to perform the duties we are obligated to carry out, but not make decisions or act on matters without the authority of applicable laws and our Governing Documents

AAM - Associated Asset Management is our general management company. The role of AAM, starting with the General Manager, Brad Phillips, is to oversee the operations of the community. Their focus is on personnel management and customer service.

TROON manages our golf courses and personnel. They provide expert information on course conditions, national standards, and service.


Through AAM and Troon, we have access to personnel in their departments such as HR, finances, IT, attorneys, their vendors (to leverage purchasing power) and others with expertise to help us in many areas. The Board is charged with overseeing the management companies, approving all salaries/benefits, services, etc.

## 2. What are Sun City’s “governing documents” and where can I find them?

All of our governing documents can be found on "Resident Central" online. Scroll down to the block that says "Governance". Click on "Governing Documents". Click on “Governing Documents” on this next page to see an explanation of what each governing documents covers and “means” to you as a resident. You will see CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions and Restrictions), By-Laws, Design Guidelines, Schedule of Fees and Community Rules, and Policies. Each of these “tabs” outline what residents agree to abide by when they purchase a home in Sun City Hilton Head.

CC&Rs are the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions. This is a legal document filed with both county recorder's offices and made a part of the official real estate records that run with the land that is part of the community. Our CC&Rs cover the rights and obligations of the homeowner’s association to its members and vice versa.

The By-laws state how Sun City Hilton Head will be run and contains information on voting processes, quorum requirements, provision for holding meetings, election requirements, and other operating guidelines.

The Design Guidelines outline what modifications residents may make to the outside of their home and in their yard. Residents really should become familiar with these and understand what is permitted within our community. NOTE: There is a separate document for Riverbend Design Guidelines (mostly with regard to new builds there), and the villas have special conditions listed in the appendix.

The Schedule of Fees and Community Rules specify the rules we expect residents to follow in the community, the fees assessed, and categories for citations associated with monetary fines and loss of amenity privileges. These are revised annually and distributed to all homeowners in late January as a hard copy.

## 4. What is the difference between an "operating fund", a "capital expense fund" and a "reserve fund"? 

Where does the money come from for each of these funds?

The "Operating Fund" covers expenses such as utilities, salaries/benefits, landscaping services, insurance, amenity maintenance/cleaning, security services, consultants, etc. This is like what you may set aside for your monthly budget as a homeowner. Basically, this fund covers expenses to keep things going day to day.

The "Capital Expense Fund" is money set aside to pay for "something new" that we might want to add to our community/amenities. In the past, these funds were used to add heaters and lighting to the outdoor Lakehouse pool, additions to the New River Sports Park, heaters for the Pavilion, new driving range, enhancements to the Argent Lakes golf course, etc. For 2025, we will fund a shade structure at Veteran’s Park over the picnic tables, and electrical outlets in two rows at the RV park then assist funding the Wood Workers expansion. As a homeowner this would be equivalent to you saving for new things to add to your home such as: a lanai, a new car, new furniture, etc..

The "Reserve Fund" is like a "savings account". This money is invested and used for renovations and restorations of current amenities/grounds. In the past we have used these funds to replace all AV/Lighting at Magnolia Hall, reroof buildings, renovate pools, purchase new furniture, renovate the interiors of buildings, repair roads/drainage/sidewalks, replace vehicles for staff and security, replace equipment, upgrade electrical systems, convert to LED lighting, repair/replace gates, etc. In 2025 we will renovate Okatie Creek Golf Course and provide other upgrades to some amenities as needed. As a homeowner, this would be like you saving for a "rainy day" expense that you know will come sometime in the future - replacing a roof, hot water heater, appliances, lawn, etc.

Money for all three funds comes from a variety of sources. The annual assessment per rooftop accounts for 64 % of our revenue, golf revenue is 15 %, and the rest comes from vendor passes, fines, fees, fitness activities, lifestyles activities, etc. Again $ 504 from each rooftop assessment goes directly to the reserve fund. In addition, every resale starting January 202 5 will generate $ 3500 for the reserve fund (example if 540 resales occur in 202 5 , at $ 3500 /resale = $1, 89 0,000). Pulte as the developer does not contribute to any of our fund accounts.

## 5. What is "transition" and when will it happen in Sun City Hilton Head? 

"Transition" is the time in the span of a Homeowners Association (HOA) when the developer relinquishes their control over the HOA and turns control over to an elected Board of Resident Directors. Currently Pulte holds three of the five seats on our Board of Directors - at transition they will relinquish those seats to the residents (as noted in our CC&Rs).

Currently Pulte is projected to build/sell 10,614 homes across the four campuses, Sun City West, Sun City North, Riverbend and Sun City South. As of 1 /3 1 /2 5 they have closed/sold 9433 homes, with 1181 left to build/sell. However, with the addition of Sun City West – there is no estimated date of transition. This of course depends on the overall economy, building rates, etc. When Pulte is finished building here, they will turn over control of the community to a Board made up of all residents. Will that Board be five members or seven or nine? That will be up to the community to decide at that time.

## 7. What new amenities can be added to Sun City Hilton Head, and will Pulte pay for them?

We can add anything the residents wish to fund and assuming we can find space for items. Currently, the Association only owns open land between Pinckney Hall and the Pavilion and land next to the new pickleball courts in Sun City North. There are very limited patches of land for use for new amenities. In December 2021 we acquired the deed to Palmetto Commons and the 10 lots around it – that now all belongs to the Community Association.

Remember, any new items we wish to add to our amenities or build here would have to be funded from the annual assessment as "capital" projects which will increase the HOA assessment. Pulte has no plans currently to fund more amenities until they build in East Argent. The Board commissioned a “Capital Improvement Task Force” in 2022 and a comprehensive study of current amenities and future needs was compiled. Any amenity we add as a Community Association must consider the initial cost, the costs for maintenance and the costs to include it in future reserves.

## 8. Is golf an amenity or a “business? I was told when I bought here that golf is self-sustaining so only golfers pay for the upkeep. Is this true or not?

Golf is a separate cost center for our Association. We can't speak to what residents were told when they purchased here, but golf is part of our community and will continue to be supported by our assessments in operating costs, capital expenses and through the reserve fund. Starting with the 202 3 fiscal year, the budget was built as a “zero based” budget.

# Basic information about Sun City Hilton Head:

## What is an “HOA”?

An HOA is a planned development that requires owners to join the Community Association, pay its fees to help cover costs for the upkeep of common areas, roads, shared structures, facilities, etc.

## When did Del Webb break ground and begin construction in SCHH? 

June 1993, Del Webb Communities, Inc. purchased 5,100 acres from the Union Camp Co & began construction in April 1994.

## When did the FIRST residents move into Sun City Hilton Head?

SCHH opened its gates in June 1995, and the first 100 residents moved into their new homes that August.

Sun City sprawl over two counties (Beaufort and Jasper): We have over more than 5,760 acres (9 square miles) + land in Sun City West.

We have 3 golf courses:
Hidden Cypress and Okatie Creek Golf Courses combined, account for 320 acres with approximately 6 miles of golf paths per course. Argent Lakes Golf Course covers 77 acres. The three courses cover ~ 397 acres.

## How many lagoons do we have across in our community? How many can be “lowered” during a hurricane event?

There are over 280 lagoons in Sun City Hilton Head (more added with each neighborhood). Technically – none can be lowered. ****There are 4 gates on the South side capable of “releasing water”, but only when circumstances are conducive. The release of water can never be relied upon to mitigate flooding. When asked by residents if the ponds can be lowered, to avoid creating a false sense of security, the answer from Common Area Maintenance is always “no”. There
are too many variables to consider before opening the gate valves.
